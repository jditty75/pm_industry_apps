# Level 1: the executive message

Overview's job (approved): *What are customers telling us about this portfolio, how much of the portfolio is that, what's coming, and what needs someone now?*

The executive message answers that question in **one figure and three lines**, before any region, list or control. It is generated by fixed rules from visible evidence. **It is not AI**, and every word in it can be traced to a figure shown on the page or in the disclosure.

## 1. Anatomy

```
┌───────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               │ A  Workday-led customer satisfaction is strong and stable.                   │  20px/600
│     85%       │ B  44 of 52 responses rated their deployment 4 or 5 · 40 deployments ·       │  14px
│  rated 4 or 5 │    Oct 2025 – Sep 2026                                                        │
│  (Top-2 Box)  │ C  2 deployments raised customer concerns in the last 90 days. Next survey   │  14px
│               │    round 4 Nov: 9 deployments to prepare by 21 Oct.                          │
└───────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   anchor figure     A = verdict · B = evidence · C = management implication
```

| Part | Answers | Content | Never contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor figure** | How satisfied? | Workday-led Top-2 Box %, with the caption "rated 4 or 5 (Top-2 Box)". In the limited and insufficient evidence tiers it becomes the response count | A mean, NPS, a second large number |
| **A: Verdict** | What does it mean? | Satisfaction word plus direction word, in scope language | Causes, comparisons, partner-led values |
| **B: Evidence** | How much should I trust it? | x of n responses · d deployments · window. Optionally an evidence-gap clause or a stale-data clause (§5) | A coverage % based on the untrusted due denominator |
| **C: Implication** | What needs someone, and what is coming? | At most two clauses, chosen by the priority rule (§4). The counts are links | Lists, names, more than two clauses |

**Five-second reading** (Healthy): *"85% — strong and stable — 2 deployments with concerns — next round 4 Nov, prepare by 21 Oct."* The reader gets the figure, the verdict, the count of concerns and the deadline without reading any region below.

## 2. Inputs (all visible somewhere on the page)

| Input | Definition | Source rule |
|---|---|---|
| `t2b` | Top-2 Box = responses with DSAT 4–5 ÷ responses with DSAT, in scope and window, **response-weighted**. Shown as a whole-number % (round half up) | M1, J1 |
| `n`, `d` | Responses with DSAT; distinct deployments behind them | J2 |
| Scope | Workday-led (default) · Partner-led · All deployments | P4 |
| Window | Rolling 12 months by default | P5 |
| Halves | Most recent 6 months vs the preceding 6, within the window. `n1`, `n2`, `t2b1`, `t2b2` | M9 |
| `concernsRecent` | Deployments with ≥1 Detractor response (DSAT ≤3 or NPS ≤6) received in the last 90 days | M5, V3-D4 |
| Round | Next survey round open date (first Wednesday); prepare-by = open − 14 days; deployments forecast; readiness issues | GB-08/17/18 |
| Chase | Open surveys, round close date, surveys to chase (all bounced, or closing ≤7 days with no response) | GB-24/25 |
| Evidence gaps | Surveys closed without response (InFlight window) + launch not seen | PQ9 |

The headline measure combines MDS and PGL responses. They ask the same DSAT question (measurement §4). The per-survey split sits in the learning row and the disclosure. **Validation:** reconcile the combined and per-survey figures with Qualtrics Key Metrics for one app/month before release (an existing V1 prerequisite).

## 3. Deterministic vocabulary

### 3.1 Evidence tier (decided first; it controls everything else)

| Tier | Condition | Anchor figure | Satisfaction word | Direction word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `NONE` | n = 0 | "0" + caption "responses in this window" | none | none |
| `INSUFFICIENT` | 1 ≤ n ≤ 4 | n + caption "responses: too few to summarise" | none | none |
| `LIMITED` | 5 ≤ n ≤ 9 | n + caption "responses" | none | none |
| `SUFFICIENT` | n ≥ 10 | `t2b`% + caption "rated 4 or 5 (Top-2 Box)" | allowed | allowed if §3.3 |

The n < 5 rule is the existing M8 suppression. The 10 boundary matches the existing direction and NPS minimums (V3-D2).

### 3.2 Satisfaction word (SUFFICIENT only; V3-D1)

| Word | Condition on displayed `t2b` |
|---|---|
| **strong** | ≥ 80% |
| **mixed** | 65–79% |
| **concerning** | < 65% |

The word comes from Top-2 Box alone. Concern counts, NPS, delivery ratings and coverage never change it. Each of those has its own sentence or region, so there is no hidden composite. The thresholds are DM product rules: the guidebook sets no target. The method note says so.

### 3.3 Direction word (SUFFICIENT only)

| Word | Condition |
|---|---|
| *(none)* + B gets "too few responses to tell direction" | `n1` < 10 or `n2` < 10 |
| **stable** | \|`t2b2` − `t2b1`\| < 10 points |
| **improving** / **declining** | Change ≥ +10 / ≤ −10 points, **and** no survey (MDS, PGL) with ≥ 10 responses in each half moved ≥ 10 points the opposite way |
| **direction unclear** | The combined figure moved ≥ 10 points, but a survey with enough data moved ≥ 10 points the opposite way (a change in the MDS/PGL mix, not a change in sentiment) |

### 3.4 Sentence A templates

`{Scope}` = "Workday-led customer satisfaction" · "Partner-led customer satisfaction" · "Customer satisfaction across all deployments".

| Tier / words | Sentence A |
|---|---|
| SUFFICIENT, word + direction agree or stable | "{Scope} is {word} and {stable/improving/declining}." |
| SUFFICIENT, opposed (strong + declining, concerning + improving) | "{Scope} is {word} but {direction}." |
| SUFFICIENT, no direction | "{Scope} is {word}." |
| SUFFICIENT, unclear | "{Scope} is {word}; direction is unclear." |
| LIMITED | "Limited {scope-adjective} evidence so far: {n} responses from {d} deployments." |
| INSUFFICIENT | "Too few {scope-adjective} responses to judge satisfaction yet." |
| NONE | "No {scope-adjective} responses in this window yet." |

"Agree" means strong + improving/stable, mixed + any direction, or concerning + declining/stable. ("concerning and stable" is allowed and truthful.)

### 3.5 Sentence B templates

| Tier | Sentence B |
|---|---|
| SUFFICIENT | "{x} of {n} responses rated their deployment 4 or 5 · {d} deployments · {window range}" + ", too few responses to tell direction" when §3.3 applies |
| LIMITED | "{x} of {n} rated their deployment 4 or 5. Too few responses to describe satisfaction or its direction (10 needed)." |
| INSUFFICIENT | "{n} responses from {d} deployments. At least 5 are needed before results are summarised." |
| NONE | "Surveys due in this window have not produced responses yet." (+ evidence-gap clause) |

## 4. Sentence C: management implication (priority rule)

C has **at most two clauses**. The concern clause is always one of them, because Level 1 must always say how many customers raised concerns, even when the answer is none.

1. **Time-critical clause** (V3-D7), first that applies:
   - Preparation closes in ≤ 7 days and readiness issues > 0 → "Preparation closes in {k} days: {m} deployments launch surveys on {date}, {r} with readiness issues."
   - The open round closes in ≤ 7 days and chase > 0 → "{c} open surveys need chasing before the round closes on {date}."
2. **Concern clause** (always):
   - concernsRecent > 0 → "{k} deployment(s) raised customer concerns in the last 90 days" + (Workday-led, any follow-up expected) ", {f} with follow-up expected".
   - concernsRecent = 0 → "No customer concerns in the last 90 days."
3. **Horizon clause** (used when no time-critical clause applies):
   - "Next survey round {date}: {m} deployments to prepare by {prepareBy}." If readiness issues = 0: "…{m} deployments, no readiness issues found."
   - No forecast in horizon: "No surveys forecast for the next two rounds."

Order: time-critical clause, then concern clause. Otherwise concern clause, then horizon clause. This is the **only** way message priority changes between states. The layout never changes.

## 5. Optional clauses (appended to B, at most one)

| Clause | When | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence gap | Tier is LIMITED, INSUFFICIENT or NONE, and evidence gaps > 0 | "{g} surveys closed without a response in the last 3 months." |
| Stale data | Responses import older than 14 days (V8 proposal) | "⚠ Responses last imported {k} days ago." |

When both apply, stale data wins. The evidence gap is still visible in the learning row.

## 6. Language rules

- **Allowed words:** strong, mixed, concerning, stable, improving, declining, direction unclear, limited evidence, too few responses, raised customer concerns, follow-up expected, readiness issues, need chasing.
- **Banned on Overview:** because, due to, driven by, caused, impact, significant, outperform, gap (as a comparison), better/worse than partners, at risk, unhappy, overdue, completed, closed (for follow-up), ready, healthy (as a verdict), score (for Top-2 Box).
- "Concern" always means a Detractor response (programme rule). The disclosure says this in one line: "A customer concern is a Detractor response: satisfaction 3 or lower, or NPS 6 or lower."
- "Remains" is not used: it implies a prior state that the sentence does not show.
- Numbers in A–C are digits. Dates are "4 Nov" in the current year and "4 Nov 2027" otherwise.
- No sentence names a deployment. Names start at Level 2.
