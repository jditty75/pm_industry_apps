# Workday Visual Guidelines

Reference for all agents specifying visual requirements in creative briefs and contracts.

---

## Visual Identity Foundation

At its core, our visual system is built on optimism and aspiration. We bring this to life visually through championing the work day and elevating our customers. Marking the start of a bright new day, sun-drenched with possibility and a rising sense of opportunity.

Our visual identity is optimistic at its core—shining a light on what makes work, work better. It brings comfort and closeness that gives decision-makers the confidence they need to navigate complexity at scale.

### Logo Clear Space

When using our logo, allow breathing room for maximum impact and legibility. Maintain a defined amount of space around the logo to prevent it from becoming cluttered by surrounding artwork, images, messaging, or the edge of a composition.

**Clear Space Rules:**
- Establish a mandatory padding/margin zone around the logo where no other visual elements (text, graphics, borders) can intrude
- The clear space zone should be proportional to the logo size
- Scale and proportion are determined by available space, aesthetics, function, and visibility
- There are no predetermined sizes—use judgment based on context

### Workday Dub

**Usage Contexts:**

- Promotional materials where space is constrained

**Dub Clear Space:**

Maintain a defined amount of space around the Dub to prevent it from becoming cluttered by surrounding artwork, images, messaging, or the edge of a composition. The same clear space principles that apply to the full logo apply to the Dub.

### Logo Colorways

To maintain a consistent brand appearance, only use approved logo and Dub variations. The color combinations detailed here have suitable contrast for use across a wide variety of media and formats.

The full-color logo is our primary logo. It is recommended for most applications and should be used as often as possible to maximize recognition, association, and memorability. Use the primary version on backgrounds that do not impair its legibility or impact.

When placing our logo on an image, ensure it has appropriate space and consider the logo's contrast against the background color. Choose a logo version to suit the needs of the given image.

| Colorway | Components | Background Colors |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Primary** | Ink text + Thumbtack sunrise | Paper (white), Keyboard |
| **Reversed** | Paper text + Thumbtack sunrise | Ink, After Hours (dark backgrounds) |
| **Ink** | All Ink | Blue Sky, Highlighter, Pencil, Lunch Break (light blue/warm backgrounds) |
| **White** | All Paper (white) | Water Cooler, Ballpoint, Ink (medium-dark backgrounds) |

**Colorway Selection Logic:**
- **Primary:** Default choice for light/white backgrounds
- **Reversed:** Use when background is dark blue (Ink or After Hours)
- **Ink:** Use on light blue or warm-colored backgrounds where the full-color logo would lack contrast
- **White:** Use on medium-blue or dark backgrounds where high contrast is needed

### Logo on Gradients

When using the logo against gradient backgrounds, consider in what part of the gradient the logo is being placed. Follow the same rules as for solid colors. The logo color must change based on the local background color behind it, not the average color of the whole image.

**Gradient Placement Rules:**
- **Dark Blue section:** Use Reversed Logo (Paper text + Thumbtack sunrise)
- **Medium Blue section:** Use White Logo (all Paper/white)
- **Yellow/Orange/Light Blue section:** Use Ink Logo (all Ink/dark blue)
- **Light-colored section:** Use Primary Logo (Ink text + Thumbtack sunrise)

### Logo Placement

In a standard piece of brand communication, the logo can be centered above or below text, or placed in any of the corners. It can also be placed in the center (for instance, a film endcard).

Exact placement of the logo will be dictated by the grid pertaining to the piece of communication, with respect to the clear-space rules.

**Valid Anchor Points:**
1. Top-Left Corner
2. Top-Right Corner
3. Bottom-Left Corner
4. Bottom-Right Corner
5. Top-Center (horizontally centered, aligned to top margin)
6. Bottom-Center (horizontally centered, aligned to bottom margin)
7. Dead Center (vertical and horizontal center—e.g., for endcards)

### Logo Minimum Size
- **Digital:** 102px width
- **Print/PDF:** 1" or 25mm or 72px width

### Logo Misuse (NEVER DO)

It is important to maintain the integrity of our brand by using the logo correctly and consistently. Do not attempt to redraw any elements of our identity or combine it with any other brand elements. Do not add any extra elements to the logo.

**Prohibited Actions:**
1. **Do not substitute:** Do not change the text (e.g., "workdaze" instead of "workday")
2. **Do not isolate the arc:** Do not use the sunrise arc alone without the text (unless using the official Workday Dub)
3. **Do not remove the arc:** Do not use the "workday" text without the sunrise arc
4. **Do not rearrange:** Do not move the arc to the bottom or change the relative position of elements
5. **Do not embed in text:** Do not use the logo as a word inside a sentence (e.g., "Workday is designed to...")
6. **Do not distort:** Do not stretch, compress, or condense the aspect ratio
7. **Do not co-brand improperly:** Do not place the logo inside other shapes or use with copyrighted materials
8. **Do not decorate:** Do not add illustrations (gears, bikes, climbers, etc.) interacting with the logo
9. **Do not redraw:** Do not sketch, hand-draw, or try to recreate the typeface—use the official asset only

### Co-Branding Logo Lockups

When determining the positioning and clear space for co-branding, use the following lockup guidance.

**Vertical Lockup:**
- Logos should be separated by the height of our "w"
- Scale partner logos proportionally to the width of the Workday logo
- Use a horizontal 2px stroke rule to clearly differentiate logos

**Horizontal Lockup:**
- Logos should be separated by the height of our "w"
- Scale partner logos optically to balance the prominence of the Workday logo
- Use a thin horizontal rule (2px) to clearly differentiate logos

### Customer Use

Customers can create their own materials to promote Workday internally, and it must be clear they are independent from Workday. Customers may not include content that leads others to believe it comes from or is endorsed by Workday.

| Treatment | Application |

### Photographic Filters
- **Dark:** For dark/contrasted images
- **Mid:** For balanced images, adds warmth
- **Bright:** For cool/bright images

### Stock Photography Tips

When selecting stock photography, follow these guidelines:

- **Avoid too-dark photos:** Black shadows in a photo cannot be recovered when working with stock photography
- **Avoid overexposed lighting:** Pure white areas cannot be recovered, just like black shadows
- **Avoid pre-filtered photos:** Do not select photos that have already been treated or adjusted with a filter
- **Avoid blue-dominant palettes:** Avoid photos with a primary color palette of blue hues
- **Seek golden-hour lighting:** Lean toward natural, golden-hour lighting for a warm, cohesive look
- **Look for depth:** Seek depth through lighting contrast to enhance image quality

### Photography Misuse (NEVER DO)

- Strictly avoid stereotypical imagery and imagery that lacks optimism
- Avoid imagery where people appear overly serious or stern
- Avoid showing backs of people
- Avoid images of obscured faces
- Avoid overly harsh shadows
- Avoid generating AI-prominent imagery
- Avoid photos that seem inauthentic or staged

## Iconography

### Grid and Margin

Icons are designed on a 56×56 pixel grid. We use a 4px margin on all sides of the grid. No element of the icons should extend into this margin.

### Focal Area

To ensure relative scale across the set, we use a circular focal area of 44px diameter placed center in the grid. This focal area is a flexible element that should contain the visual bulk of the icon and limit overshoot outside up to the margin, only when necessary.

### Stroke

Consistent line quality is critical for creating cohesive icons.

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| **Stroke weight** | Always 2px |
| **Terminals** | Custom semi-rounded arrowhead element (see Arrowheads section) |
| **Corners** | Round join—gives a soft outer edge and sharp interior |

### Horizon Curve

When constructing new icons, always include a use of the horizon curve.

- The horizon should span the width of the icon and is always centered
- The curve can be oriented either up or down
- Limit designs to one clear horizon curve (two maximum)
- The curve should always feel like a natural part of the design—not forced
- Some designs may not work; in some instances, more abstract use than literal might be required

### Arrowheads

To achieve the semi-rounded terminals, apply a custom arrowhead on every stroke to both the beginning and end.

**Installation (Adobe Illustrator):**
1. Open: Applications > Adobe Illustrator > Plug-ins
2. Drag the latest arrowhead file version into the Plug-ins folder
3. Restart Illustrator
4. Access arrowhead settings in the additional options of the stroke panel

**Alignment Settings:**
- **Vertical and horizontal strokes:** Set "Align: Arrow tip at end of path"
- **Angled or curved strokes:** Set "Align: Extend arrow tip beyond end of path" (note: this will make the terminal slightly longer; adjust spacing between elements if necessary)

### Spacing

We utilize a scale of 2px increments for spacing with a 2px minimum between elements.

- In some cases 3px or 5px may be necessary but should be limited as much as possible
- Priority should be given to form
- Within all icons, include gaps between major forms to add dimension
- Use these gaps to simplify the icon in places where a connection of lines may become overly dense or complicated
- Consider placing gaps between major construction lines of forms or in places of naturally occurring shadow to create planes

### Icon Misuse (NEVER DO)

Consistency is paramount in establishing a recognizable brand throughout our communications. Avoid the following treatments:

- Do not color individual elements separately
- Do not use an icon unrelated to the subject matter
- Do not place over busy imagery
- Do not stretch or warp the icons in any way
- Do not create icons without gaps or horizon curves
- Avoid creating overly literal, complex icons

## Forever Forward Usage

The phrase "Forever forward" has specific visual rules:

| Rule | Guidance |
|------|----------|
| **Capitalization** | "Forever forward" (lowercase f on second word) |
| **In sentences** | "Let's move forever forward." |
| **As phrase** | "Forever forward" (not "Forever Forward") |
| **With logo** | Position under Workday logo, or insert next to "Workday" in text |
| **No trademark** | Never use ™ symbol |
| **No graphics** | Don't add icons or graphics to the phrase |
| **No italics** | Don't italicize in body copy |

### Correct Examples
- Workday. Forever forward.
- Let's move forever forward.
- For your people. For your money. Forever forward.

### Incorrect Examples
- ~~Forever Forward~~ (wrong caps)
- ~~Forever forward™~~ (no trademark)
- ~~Workday Forever forward.~~ (needs period after Workday)
